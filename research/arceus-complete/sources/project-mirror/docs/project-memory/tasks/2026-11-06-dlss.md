## Material motion and cut isolation, November 6 evening

The DLSS and FSR test launchers now use the same split-resolution candidate 53379fc93072642d96e242cfbafd246d83d71e763648753c5a237d10937c71d8. The separate native TAA candidate is 475e4aecaeb3d765225352a3f3ec7a2c7fba64a4ca4fa28405fc7ab0b8c4b09b. The stock B32 executable is still installed. No gameplay session was started.

Opaque material indices from deferred texture word 0x2e are compared with the adjacent frame's packed color, reverse depth and material. The Vulkan estimator uses camera-relative material motion, overlapping spatial partitions, consistency votes and subpixel color refinement. Ambiguous textured surfaces can retain a camera match. Projection jitter is excluded from the vectors. Missing/stale surfaces, cold history or an unavailable estimator fall back to the camera path. No native skin/particle velocity buffer was identified. The 64-pixel correspondence search is deliberately bounded.

The RTX 5090 ran the actual integrated Vulkan graph with three backends. Over five sampled frames of a jittered walk, stop and reversal, mean character RGB difference from each backend using exact motion decreased by about 47% for TAA, 57% for DLSS and 71% for FSR against the camera-only path. Native and AMD use the confidence mask. On this NGX build, its bias mask showed negligible benefit and stays unbound in the launcher. Stationary and fractional camera-only controls produced zero false background corrections over one pixel. In the opposing-copy test 99.77% of character estimates were within one input pixel, with about 0.008% of unchanged background receiving a false correction. A fast 12-degree turn still averaged 24.2 pixels of error against 43.9 from camera alone.

A single NGX reset in a controlled cut left 254 pixels with prior-scene dependence over .005 in display-mapped RGB. Re-evaluating the same current frame with reset and a Vulkan memory dependency isolated the cut and the next three evaluations exactly across unrelated histories. Ordinary frames invoke NGX once. The comparison is in fix/work/motion/cut_isolation.py and continuous/cut-isolation.json.

F3 now exports a temporal manifest, motion RG16F, confidence R8, and the two consecutive packed features RG32UI when history is warm. All three writers passed GPU readback checks against the original material/depth/color inputs. inspect_capture.py can visualize a real capture; check_snapshot.py checks the controlled fixture. Source and results are packaged in fix/outputs. The next manual run should capture a walking/turning NPC or player, check motion=material+camera and pre-HUD composition, and measure real frame times.
