CONFIRMED FINDINGS VS HYPOTHESIS
Confirmed:
1. The visible stripes are present in shadow-receiver output before main
   lighting/final postprocessing. Visual comparison alone does not establish
   which receiver input or arithmetic first becomes incorrect.
2. Shadow source targets are1365x1365, their producer viewport1365.333 square
   at4/3 scale, but the sampled array is1024 square.
3. All2,097,152 depth-array texels are bitwise identical to nearest-neighbor
   reduction of those two captured source images. Exact check script:
   work/check-shadow-resampling.py
   Results: outputs/arceus-shadow-resampling-evidence.json
4. This copy comes from src/nx/gpu/vk/texture.c:copy_layers. Scaled RTs are
   blitted using vk_sc(w/h) source extents into native-size texture layers,
   VK_FILTER_NEAREST. Receiver manually compares depth; its ShTex.shadow is0,
   with linear min/mag sampling. Integer tag samplers are forced nearest.
5. Captured raster depth-bias values/enables are zero. Shader-computed bias
   has NOT been ruled out or fully measured.

Current hypothesis, NOT established root cause:
Nearest reduction moves depth samples relative to receiver positions, causing
repeating self-shadowing on sloped surfaces. A local planar estimate shows
phase-dependent depth errors. It is not a replay of actual receiver comparisons,
does not know bias sufficiency, and is not proof of the full cause.
Please challenge this: inspect receiver translation, projection/reconstruction,
manual comparison/filter semantics, normal offset/bias and sampler coordinates.
Look for causes Codex missed instead of only confirming the resampling theory.
